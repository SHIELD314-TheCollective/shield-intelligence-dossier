Our Read: This lands directly on your wire and ACH screening, not just your crypto desk.

2. Top Signals & Typology Breakdown

Deep-dive analysis on active threats, mechanics, pivot keys, and frontline impacts

Mechanism & Vector

Treasury announced the order under Operation Economic Outcast (FR Doc. 2026-22962) using Section 9714 authority. FinCEN identified MEXC Global as a primary money laundering concern tied to Russian, Iranian, and North Korean illicit finance ($16B+ from May 2021 to May 2026). The order bans any transmittal of funds from or to MEXC Global (taking effect ~Dec 10, 2026) while maintaining existing BSA and OFAC obligations.

Pivot Keys

  • Target Name: MEXC Global and aliases/wallets
  • Effective window: ~30 days post Federal Register publication
  • Fiat touchpoints: Wire/ACH transfers connected to exchange cash-outs

Frontline Impact

Request analytics vendor lists for MEXC counterparties, review past 12 months of wires/ACH, and set up screening blocks ahead of the effective date.

Source: Treasury Press Release sb0685 & FinCEN, Nov 6, 2026; Federal Register FR Doc. 2026-22962. Treasury Release sb0685

Mechanism & Vector

SDNY unsealed charges against Jacob Soall, Sharon Hazan, and Guy Sheetrit for a $129M deepfake CEO/lawyer fraud scheme operating from July 2024 to February 2026. Fraudsters used AI voice and face cloning during video/audio calls, guiding finance staff with scripts (e.g., "Lawyer Call") to route urgent confidential deal wires into international receiving accounts before converting funds to crypto.

Pivot Keys

  • Aliases noted in reports: "Rocky Balboa", "Superman Cash", "Fat Boy"
  • "Lawyer Call" scripting framing confidential M&A/deals
  • First-time international wires to Europe rapidly moving to crypto

Frontline Impact

Require secondary callback verification on pre-existing numbers for large corporate wires. Initiate same-day recall and IC3 notifications for affected corporate clients.

Source: AP / ITV & Greek City Times SDNY Report, Nov 6–8, 2026. Greek City Times SDNY Detail

Mechanism & Vector

Elliptic's Nov 6 update shows ~$6.43B stolen from Binance, with ~$2.85B (44%) frozen and ~$3.11B remaining in linked addresses (mostly ETH and BTC). Attackers utilized 20,000+ wallets across 16 blockchains and 36 services. TRM Labs (Nov 3) highlighted three advanced laundering tricks: self-minted tokens paired with lending receipt tokens, receipt-token relays bypassing standard USDT traces, and chained cross-chain swaps via THORChain.

Pivot Keys

  • Receipt-token transfers between isolated intermediary wallets
  • ETH chunking addresses splitting capital into 50–80 ETH blocks
  • THORChain cross-chain bridge activity

Frontline Impact

Maintain strict analytics exposure flags on inbound exchange and OTC wires, documenting source and purpose for all unfamiliar fiat credits.

Source: Decrypt / Elliptic & TRM Labs, Nov 3–6, 2026. Decrypt Elliptic Report

Mechanism & Vector

Block disclosed a $964M agreement in principle with DOJ ($764M penalty plus $200M prior credit) under a 3-year DPA addressing legacy BSA deficiencies. Concurrently, FINRA fined Webull $4.2M for AML and ID theft prevention failures (including an uninvestigated backlog of ~13,000 alerts), and UBS paid $125M to U.S. agencies led by FinCEN over historical CDD failures.

Pivot Keys

  • Alert aging backlogs exceeding internal policy thresholds
  • Customer due diligence files lacking scheduled periodic refreshes
  • Single-source or weak ID verification during onboarding

Frontline Impact

Audit your oldest 25 open alerts and top 10 noisiest accounts, ensuring documented rationale for all pending reviews.

Source: Banking Dive, Nov 4, 2026; Zigram AML Digest, Nov 9, 2026. Banking Dive Block Report

3. Frontline Red Flags & Queue Indicators

Filter operational indicators across banking and compliance workflows

Branch / Teller Line
  • Customer asking to wire to/from an exchange mentioning MEXC; halt pending compliance review.
  • Business owner/employee claiming CEO or lawyer called about a confidential deal requesting a wire.
  • Customer inquiring about "recovery services" or fake FBI/FTC agents following crypto losses.
New Accounts / Onboarding
  • New business accounts for consulting, trading, or legal services expecting large inbound wires from abroad with thin web presence.
  • Applicants describing business models around moving crypto for others without proper licensing.
Digital Onboarding / IDV
  • Live video verification streams that appear overly smooth, lag, or break during head-turn checks.
  • Multiple applications from single device/IP address utilizing distinct identities and proxy/VPN masking.
Deposit Ops / ACH / Wire
  • Large first-time international wires requested via email/phone citing urgency and confidential deals.
  • Any wire or ACH transmittal involving MEXC Global or associated wallet addresses post-effective date.
  • Credits from unfamiliar OTC desks followed by immediate outbound transfers.
Online Banking / TM Rules
  • First outbound transfer within 24 hours of new device enrollment with active RAT flags or call sessions.
  • Open alert backlogs exceeding policy aging limits and repeat alerts left uninvestigated.
BSA / SAR / Sanctions
  • MEXC Global: Integrate names, aliases, and vendor lists into screening; document handling of existing accounts.
  • AI Impersonation: Initiate same-day recall and IC3 steps; explicitly note voice/video cloning details in SAR narratives.

4. Aftercare & Human Risk Protocol

Human-stack risk management, victim care, and operational boundary protocols

No-Blame Support for Compromised Staff

In AI impersonation cases, employees interact with authentic-sounding "CEOs" and follow standard-looking procedures. Meet them quickly, thank them for reporting, and maintain focus on control gaps rather than personal blame.

SAR Confidentiality Boundaries

Staff may discuss underlying facts, note that delays relate to suspected fraud or sanctions rules, and give mule warnings. Staff **may not** disclose that a SAR exists or was filed. Use the Sept 2 joint statement guidelines.

Closing Re-Victimization Doors

Following high-profile fraud news, scammers pose as FBI, IC3, law firms, or recovery services charging upfront fees. Inform intake clients that no legitimate agency charges recovery fees.

Protecting the Front Line

Branch and wire staff executing complex sanctions blocks and fraud refusals carry heavy stress. Provide clear scripting, clarify accountability protections, and hold brief team debriefs following hard customer interactions.
